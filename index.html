Measuring bias instead of assuming it

This study investigates how the limited skin-tone diversity of public dermoscopic datasets — ISIC, HAM10000 and PH2, which skew heavily toward Fitzpatrick I–III — affects the reliability of melanoma classification.

Rather than inferring bias from dataset composition, the method measures it directly. Uniform tone shifts are applied in Lab space and reported as ITA, holding lesion geometry and local contrast constant, so any change in the model's output is attributable to skin tone alone.

The result is a systematic shift toward false reassurance as skin darkens. That is the harmful direction: melanoma on darker skin is already diagnosed later and at worse stages, and the application serves a population that is largely Fitzpatrick III–V. The finding is therefore documented openly rather than quietly patched.
